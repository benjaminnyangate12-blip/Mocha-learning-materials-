BCS 116: ELECTRICAL PRINCIPLES

Detailed Theory and Practice Notes

Electrical principles explain how electric charge, current, voltage, and power behave in circuits. These concepts are central to all electrical and electronic systems used in homes, schools, industries, and modern technology.

1. Fundamentals of Electricity

Electricity is the movement of electric charges through a conducting medium. The flow of charge is current, while the force driving the charges is voltage.

1.1 Basic Electrical Quantities

QuantitySymbolUnitMeaning
CurrentIAmpere (A)Rate of flow of electric charge
VoltageVVolt (V)Potential difference driving the current
ResistanceROhm (Ω)Opposition to current
PowerPWatt (W)Rate at which energy is used
EnergyEJoule (J)Total work done

2. Ohm’s Law

V = IR

It shows the relationship between voltage, current, and resistance in a conductor.

Example: If V = 12V and R = 4Ω, then I = V/R = 3A.

3. Electrical Circuits

An electrical circuit is a complete path along which electric current can flow. It usually contains a source, conductors, control switches, and a load.

3.1 Series Circuits

In a series circuit, components are connected end-to-end, so the same current flows through each component.

R_total = R1 + R2 + R3

3.2 Parallel Circuits

Parallel circuits provide multiple paths, and the same voltage is applied to each branch.

1/R_total = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3

4. Power and Energy

Power is the rate at which electrical energy is converted or consumed.

P = VI

Other equivalent formulas include:

P = I^2R and P = V^2 / R

Energy formula: E = Pt, where t is time.

5. Conductors, Insulators and Semiconductors

TypePropertyExamples
ConductorsAllow current to flow freelyCopper, aluminum
InsulatorsPrevent or resist current flowRubber, plastic, glass
SemiconductorsConduct under some conditionsSilicon, germanium

6. Electrical Components

  • Resistor: controls current
  • Capacitor: stores electrical charge
  • Inductor: stores energy in a magnetic field
  • Switch: opens or closes a circuit
  • Fuse: prevents excessive current
  • Battery: provides DC source

7. Magnetism and Electromagnetism

Electric current produces a magnetic field. This principle is used in relays, transformers, motors, and generators.

Application: electromagnets are widely used in electrical machines and switching devices.

8. AC and DC

TypeDescriptionExample
DCCurrent flows in one directionBattery
ACCurrent direction changes continuouslyMains electricity

9. Safety in Electrical Work

Electrical systems can be dangerous if not handled properly. Safety is essential.

  • Use proper insulation
  • Never work on live circuits without protection
  • Use correct tools and protective gear
  • Do not overload sockets
  • Ensure correct earthing and grounding

10. Summary

Electrical principles form the foundation for understanding circuits and energy transfer. Concepts such as current, voltage, resistance, and power are essential in both everyday electrical applications and advanced electronic systems.
