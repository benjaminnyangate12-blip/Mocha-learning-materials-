BCS 116: ELECTRICAL PRINCIPLES

Detailed Theory and Practice Notes

Electrical principles form the basis for understanding how electricity behaves in circuits and how electrical energy is transmitted, stored, and used in practical systems.

1. Fundamentals of Electricity

Electricity is the movement of charge through a conductor. It is responsible for powering appliances, machines, lighting, and electronic systems.

1.1 Basic Electrical Quantities

QuantitySymbolUnitMeaning
CurrentIAmpere (A)Rate of flow of electric charge
VoltageVVolt (V)Potential difference driving the current
ResistanceROhm (Ω)Opposition to current flow
PowerPWatt (W)Rate at which electrical energy is used
EnergyEJoule (J)Total work done by electrical power

2. Ohm's Law

V = IR

  • Voltage is directly proportional to current when resistance is constant.
  • If resistance increases, current decreases for the same voltage.
Example: If V = 12V and R = 4Ω, then I = V/R = 12/4 = 3A.

3. Electrical Circuits

An electrical circuit is a complete path for electric current to flow.

  • Source: battery or generator
  • Load: lamp, resistor, motor, or appliance
  • Conductors: wires
  • Control elements: switches and relays

3.1 Series Circuits

In a series circuit, components are connected one after another. Current is the same throughout the circuit.

R_total = R1 + R2 + R3

3.2 Parallel Circuits

In a parallel circuit, components are connected across the same voltage source. Voltage is the same across each branch.

1/R_total = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3

4. Power and Energy

Power in a circuit can be calculated using:

P = VI

Alternative forms include:

P = I^2R and P = V^2/R

Energy: E = Pt, where t is time in seconds.

5. Conductors, Insulators, and Semiconductors

TypeBehaviorExamples
ConductorsAllow current to flow easilyCopper, aluminum
InsulatorsResist current flowRubber, glass, plastic
SemiconductorsHave intermediate conductivitySilicon, germanium

6. Electrical Components

  • Resistor: limits or controls current
  • Capacitor: stores electrical energy
  • Inductor: stores energy in a magnetic field
  • Switch: opens or closes a circuit
  • Fuse: protects circuit from excessive current
  • Battery: provides direct current

7. Magnetism and Electromagnetism

Magnetism is closely related to electricity. A current-carrying wire creates a magnetic field around it.

Application: electromagnets are used in motors, relays, and transformers.

7.1 Faraday's Law

Changing magnetic flux induces an electromotive force (EMF) in a conductor.

7.2 Transformer

A transformer transfers electrical energy between circuits using electromagnetic induction.

8. AC and DC

TypeDescriptionExample
Direct current (DC)Current flows in one direction onlyBattery
Alternating current (AC)Direction reverses periodicallyMains electricity

9. Safety in Electrical Work

Electrical systems can be dangerous. Safety is very important.

  • Never work on live circuits without proper insulation
  • Use correct tools and protective equipment
  • Never overload sockets and extension leads
  • Always follow grounding and earthing procedures

10. Application Areas

  • Power generation and distribution
  • Household wiring
  • Industrial control systems
  • Electronics and communication systems
  • Motor and generator technology

11. Summary

Electrical principles explain how charges, voltage, current, resistance, and power behave in circuits. These ideas are central to designing and maintaining electrical systems used in homes, industries, and modern digital technology.
