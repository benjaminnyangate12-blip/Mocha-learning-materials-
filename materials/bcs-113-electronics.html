BCS 113: ELECTRONICS

Detailed Study Notes

Electronics is the study of electrical circuits and devices that control the flow of current. It involves the use of components such as resistors, capacitors, inductors, diodes, and transistors to design useful electronic systems.

1. Introduction to Electronics

Electronics deals with the flow and control of electrons in electrical circuits. It forms the foundation of devices such as radios, computers, televisions, power supplies, and mobile phones.

3. Ohm's Law

Ohm's Law states that the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage across it and inversely proportional to its resistance.

V = IR

Example: If a resistor has a resistance of 10Ω and a current of 2A flows through it, then V = 2 × 10 = 20V.

4. Electrical Components

4.1 Resistors

A resistor limits current flow and controls voltage in a circuit.

  • Fixed resistors
  • Variable resistors (potentiometers)
  • Thermistors and LDRs

4.2 Capacitors

A capacitor stores electrical energy in an electric field.

Uses include filtering, smoothing power supply, and timing circuits.

4.3 Inductors

An inductor stores energy in a magnetic field and opposes changes in current.

4.4 Diodes

A diode allows current to flow in one direction only. It is used for rectification and protection.

Key point: A diode is like a one-way valve for electricity.

4.5 Transistors

A transistor is a semiconductor device used to amplify or switch electronic signals.

TypeUse
BJTAmplification and switching
FETHigh input impedance and switching

5. Semiconductor Materials

Materials such as silicon and germanium are used in electronics because their conductivity can be controlled.

  • Conductors: allow current to flow easily
  • Insulators: resist current flow
  • Semiconductors: have conductivity between conductors and insulators

6. Series and Parallel Circuits

6.1 Series Circuit

In a series circuit, components are connected end-to-end, so the same current flows through each component.

Vsource ── R1 ── R2 ── R3 ──

Total resistance: R_total = R1 + R2 + R3

6.2 Parallel Circuit

In a parallel circuit, components share the same voltage but divide the current.

      R1
 Vsource ─┤├── R2
      R3

Total resistance is calculated using:

1/R_total = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3

8. Power and Energy

Power is the rate at which energy is used or supplied.

P = VI

Also: P = I^2R = V^2/R

Example: If a device uses 12V and 2A, then P = 12 × 2 = 24W.

9. Semiconductors and Diodes

Semiconductor diode has a p-n junction. It allows current to flow in forward bias and blocks current in reverse bias.

  • Forward bias: diode conducts
  • Reverse bias: diode blocks current

10. Applications of Electronics

  • Power supplies
  • Telecommunication systems
  • Computers and mobile devices
  • Medical equipment
  • Control systems and automation

11. Summary

Electronics is the science of controlling electric current through devices and circuits. It relies on core principles such as voltage, current, resistance, semiconductor behavior, and circuit design. These concepts are used in modern technology and communication systems.
