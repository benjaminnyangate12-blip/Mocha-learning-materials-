BCS 113: ELECTRONICS

Detailed Study Notes

Electronics is the study of the controlled motion of electric charges through components and circuits. It is important because modern technologies such as smartphones, computers, radios, televisions, and automated systems all depend on electronic systems.

1. Introduction to Electronics

Electronics is a field of engineering and science concerned with electrical circuits and devices that use the flow and control of electrons. It differs from general electrical engineering in that it focuses more on signal processing, switching, amplification, and electronic control.

At its simplest, electronics is the science of taking a small electrical signal and using it to control larger actions or to transmit information accurately.

1.1 Significance of Electronics

  • It enables communication devices such as radios, satellites, and mobile networks.
  • It supports computing systems and memory devices.
  • It is used in medical devices and control systems.
  • It forms the foundation of automation and robotics.
  • It underlies nearly all modern digital technology.

2.1 Current

Current is the flow of electric charge through a conductor. It is measured in amperes (A). A higher current means more electrons flow past a point per second.

2.2 Voltage

Voltage is the electrical potential difference between two points. It is the driving force that pushes charge through a circuit.

2.3 Resistance

Resistance is the opposition to the flow of current. Materials with low resistance conduct easily; materials with high resistance inhibit current flow.

3. Ohm’s Law

Ohm’s law describes the relationship between voltage, current, and resistance:

V = I R

This means that the voltage across a conductor is directly proportional to the current through it and the resistance of that conductor.

Worked Example: A resistor has a resistance of 12Ω and a current of 2A flows through it. Find the voltage.
V = IR = 2 × 12 = 24V

3.1 Re-arranged Forms

I = V / R

R = V / I

Ohm’s law is fundamental in circuit analysis and is used in nearly all electronic calculations.

4. Basic Electronic Components

4.1 Resistors

Resistors limit or control current and help set operating voltages in a circuit. They are one of the most important components in electronics.

They are used to:

  • limit current
  • divide voltage
  • protect components
  • set bias in circuits

4.2 Capacitors

A capacitor stores electrical charge and releases it when needed. It can block direct current while allowing alternating current to pass depending on circuit design.

Applications include:

  • smoothing power supplies
  • timing circuits
  • filtering signals

4.3 Inductors

An inductor stores energy in a magnetic field when current flows through a coil. It resists changes in current.

Common applications include reactors, filters, transformers, and inductive sensors.

4.4 Diodes

A diode is a semiconductor device that allows current to flow in one direction and blocks it in the opposite direction.

Uses include:

  • rectification
  • protection against reverse voltage
  • signal limiting
Important: A diode behaves like a one-way valve for current.

4.5 Transistors

Transistors are the building blocks of modern electronics. They can act as switches or amplifiers.

TypeMain Function
BJTAmplification and switching
FETVoltage-controlled switching and amplification

Transistors made electronics possible because they are much smaller, more efficient, and more reliable than vacuum tubes.

5. Semiconductors

Semiconductors are materials whose electrical conductivity lies between that of conductors and insulators. Silicon is the most common semiconductor material.

Material TypeConductivityExamples
ConductorsHighCopper, aluminum
InsulatorsVery lowPlastic, glass, rubber
SemiconductorsModerate and controllableSilicon, germanium

5.1 Doping

Doping is the addition of small amounts of impurities to a semiconductor to change its electrical properties. This creates p-type and n-type semiconductors.

  • n-type: has extra electrons
  • p-type: has electron holes

6. Semiconductor Diode Operation

A diode is formed by joining p-type and n-type semiconductors. This creates a p-n junction.

ConditionEffect
Forward biasDiode conducts current
Reverse biasDiode blocks current
Practical Idea: A diode is used in power supplies to convert AC to DC in rectifier circuits.

7. Circuits

7.1 Series Circuits

In a series circuit, components are connected end-to-end, so the same current passes through each component.

R_total = R1 + R2 + R3

If R1 = 3Ω, R2 = 5Ω, R3 = 2Ω, then R_total = 10Ω.

7.2 Parallel Circuits

In a parallel circuit, components share the same voltage but the total current splits across branches.

1/R_total = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3

7.3 Mixed Circuits

Many real electronic systems combine series and parallel connections. Circuit analysis requires applying both Ohm’s law and Kirchhoff’s rules.

8. Kirchhoff's Laws

8.1 Kirchhoff’s Current Law (KCL)

The sum of currents entering a junction equals the sum of currents leaving the junction.

8.2 Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law (KVL)

The sum of all voltage rises and drops around a closed loop is zero.

These laws are crucial in analyzing complex circuits and understanding how current and voltage behave in networks.

9. Alternating Current (AC) and Direct Current (DC)

TypeCharacteristicExample

AC is used for electricity transmission over long distances because it is easier to transform efficiently.

10. Power and Energy in Electronics

Electrical power is the rate at which energy is converted or transferred.

P = V I

Using Ohm’s law, other configurations are:

P = I^2 R

P = V^2 / R

Energy is related to time by:

E = P t

Example: If a device uses 5V at 2A, then P = 10W. If used for 3 hours, then E = 10 × 3 × 3600 = 108000J.

11. Rectification and Power Supplies

A rectifier converts AC to DC. The key electronic component used is the diode.

  • Half-wave rectifier: allows only one half cycle
  • Full-wave rectifier: uses both halves of the AC cycle
  • Filter capacitor: smooths the DC output

Power supplies are used in computers, chargers, televisions, and many household appliances.

12. Transistor as Switch and Amplifier

Transistors are used in two major ways:

  • Switch: on/off control of current
  • Amplifier: increase the strength of a weak input signal

This makes them central to digital logic, audio devices, signal processing, and telecommunication systems.

13. Integrated Circuits (ICs)

An integrated circuit is a compact electronic circuit containing many transistors and other components on a single chip. Examples include microprocessors, memory chips, and operational amplifiers.

ICs changed electronics because they made devices smaller, cheaper, and more reliable.

14. Basic Logic Gates

Logic gates are the fundamental electronic circuits used in digital systems.

GateOutput
ANDTrue only if all inputs are true
ORTrue if any input is true
NOTReverses input
XORTrue if inputs differ

These are the foundations of digital electronics and computer hardware.

15. Practical Applications of Electronics

  • Computers and mobile communication
  • Medical sensors and scanners
  • Audio and video systems
  • Industrial automation and control
  • Power systems and renewable energy
  • Transportation and robotics

16. Safety in Electronic Work

Electrical and electronic systems can be dangerous, especially when dealing with high voltages and currents.

  • Always isolate power before working on a circuit.
  • Use insulated tools.
  • Check component ratings.
  • Never assume a circuit is safe without measuring it.

17. Troubleshooting Circuits

When a circuit fails, common steps include:

  1. Check power supply
  2. Inspect visible wiring and components
  3. Test continuity and voltage
  4. Verify component polarity
  5. Compare against expected values

18. Summary

Electronics is the science and technology of controlling electric current through circuits and semiconductor devices. The key principles include voltage, current, resistance, capacitance, inductance, semiconductor behavior, and circuit design. These principles are essential for understanding modern technology and digital systems.
